## Part 1 — Tyler's Origin & Operating Philosophy

I started knocking storm doors in Oklahoma in 2010 with $200 and a borrowed F-150. By 2018 I was COO of a multi-state roofing operation doing $22M with 47 reps. I didn't get there because I'm a savant. I got there because I built a system, and the system didn't care if I had a bad day.

That's the whole pitch of Roof Pitcher. Systems beat heroes. A 23-year-old rep with the right script and the right neighborhood will outclose your "best guy" who's been winging it for 10 years. Every time.

**My core beliefs about storm sales:**

1. **The homeowner is not your enemy.** They've had three guys knock today. They're tired. They're skeptical. Your job is to be the one who acts like an inspector, not a closer. The close happens later, almost by accident.

2. **You don't sell roofs. You sell the inspection.** If you walk away from a door with a booked inspection slot, you won. The sale happens at the table after the inspection, not at the door.

3. **Storms are a window, not a strategy.** The window is 14 days. After day 14, claim filing rates collapse, your competitors have saturated the market, and homeowners are exhausted. If your operation can't mobilize in 72 hours after a storm, you are leaving 70% of the available revenue on the table.

4. **Cheap pitches lose money.** Anything that sounds manipulative — fake urgency, "the adjuster missed something," scare tactics about leaks — loses you the supplement battle later and torches your reputation in tight neighborhoods. Roofing is a referral business. Burn one block, you've burned the next three.

5. **The pitch is consultative or it's nothing.** Ask questions. Listen. Take notes on a clipboard. Look like an inspector, not a salesman. Homeowners thank you for it, and they file the claim because you helped them, not because you closed them.

## Part 2 — The 7-Question Storm Knock (The Core Frame)

This is the opener I've used since 2014. We've A/B-tested it against probably 30 variations. Nothing beats it for post-storm canvassing within the 14-day window.

The structure has three phases: **Disarm, Diagnose, Decide.**

### Phase 1: Disarm (first 15 seconds)

> "Hey, I'm [Name] with Roof Pitcher. I'm not here to sell you anything — I'm doing free 60-second damage checks on this street after the storm Tuesday."

The phrase **"I'm not here to sell you anything"** is critical. Say it within the first 5 words. The homeowner's hand was on the door, ready to close it. You just gave them a reason to leave it open another 10 seconds.

The phrase **"free 60-second"** also matters. People will give a stranger 60 seconds. They will not give a stranger 5 minutes.

### Phase 2: Diagnose (the 7 questions, in this order)

You don't ask all 7 every time. You ask until you get a clear signal. Most reps stop at question 4.

1. **"Have you had anyone up there yet?"**
   - If yes → "Cool. Did they leave you a written scope?" → moves to Q3.
   - If no → moves to Q4.
2. **"Did they file a claim for you, or just give you a quote?"**
   - Identifies whether you're competing with another contractor who has already started the claim — different play.
3. **"Have you filed with insurance yet?"**
   - Most important question for storm sales. If no → you have the opening.
4. **"Mind if I take a ladder up there real quick? 60 seconds, I'll show you photos either way."**
   - This is the close at the door. You're not asking for a sale. You're asking for an inspection.
5. **"Have you noticed any leaks or stains on ceilings recently?"**
   - Use only if Q4 gets a no. Surfaces hidden damage that wasn't visible from the curb.
6. **"How old is your roof, do you know?"**
   - Sets context for the conversation. Anything 10+ years old, the storm just gave them a free new roof.
7. **"If I find damage, would you want help walking through what filing a claim looks like?"**
   - This is the consent question. Asking permission to help them later if you find damage. Always works because they have nothing to lose.

### Phase 3: Decide (the 60 seconds on the ladder)

You go up. You take 4–6 photos:
- Wide shot of the worst slope
- Closeup of 3–4 hail hits (use a chalk circle, easier to see in photos)
- Soft metals (gutters, AC fins, vents) — these almost always show hail signs
- Roof vent or pipe boot — common adjuster-missed items

You come down. You show them the photos on your phone. You **never** stand at the door and lecture. You sit on the porch step or lean on the railing. Body language matters.

You say: *"Here's what I found. Two options: file a claim and let's see what insurance says — costs you nothing — or do nothing and we'll come back in 6 months and check again. Which feels right?"*

Two options. No urgency. No fear. They decide. And 60–70% of the time they choose to file.

## Part 3 — The Objection Vault (Storm Edition)

Every objection a homeowner gives you in storm territory falls into one of 8 buckets. Learn the bucket, not the script.

### Bucket 1: "I already have a guy."

Almost never true literally. Translation: "I'm overwhelmed and don't want another decision today."

**Response:** *"Totally fair. Did the guy file the claim for you or did he just leave a quote? Because if he didn't file, you're still 100% open to whoever you want to use. The claim's between you and your insurance — not between you and a contractor."*

This reframes the situation. They thought they were committed. They weren't.

### Bucket 2: "My roof is fine."

Translation: "I haven't looked."

**Response:** *"Probably is. Most are. But hail bruises don't show from the ground — they only show on the soft metals and up close. If your gutters and AC fins are clean, we'll be off your lawn in 90 seconds. If they're not, we'll talk."*

You're inviting an inspection without asking for one. The dent on the AC fin sells the inspection by itself.

### Bucket 3: "I don't want my insurance to go up."

Translation: They've been told (incorrectly) that filing a claim raises rates.

**Response:** *"Common worry. Storm claims are non-fault, which means by law in [state] they can't single you out for a rate increase. Your whole zip's already going up next year if the storm was bad enough — claim or no claim. Want me to point you to the [state] DOI page on this so you can verify before you file?"*

You're offering proof, not pitching. Roofers who can quote DOI rules close 2x more than roofers who hand-wave it.

### Bucket 4: "I can't afford the deductible."

Translation: "I haven't done the math."

**Response:** *"How much is your deductible? [Listen.] So you're paying [$X] and you're getting a $14,000 roof. Most homeowners finance the deductible at $100/month if cash is tight. The new roof adds $8K–$15K to your home value the day it's installed. The math is heavily in your favor — want me to show you on paper?"*

Get the conversation to dollars and cents. Most homeowners haven't actually compared the deductible to the new asset value.

### Bucket 5: "I need to talk to my spouse."

Translation: Real reason 70% of the time. Stall 30% of the time.

**Response:** *"Completely fair. Best decisions get made together. Two options: I can come back tomorrow at [time] when you're both home, or I can call your spouse right now and explain what I'd be doing — your call, what's easier?"*

You make it easier for them to include their spouse, not harder. Set the next-step time before you leave.

### Bucket 6: "The adjuster already came and denied it."

Translation: They were denied or partially approved on a previous claim.

**Response:** *"Got it. Was that recent or before [storm date]? Because adjusters write what they see on a given day. After [storm date], anything new from the storm is a new claim. I'd want to take a look at what's on the roof today, not what was on the roof six months ago. Sound fair?"*

You're not arguing with the previous adjuster. You're pointing out that the world changed.

### Bucket 7: "I'll think about it."

Translation: You didn't earn the inspection.

**Response:** *"Of course. Think about what specifically? — because if it's the time investment, the inspection is 60 seconds; if it's trusting a stranger, I get it, that's why I'm leaving you the photos either way."*

Diagnose. Don't push. The right question reopens the conversation 40% of the time.

### Bucket 8: "How are you different from the other guys?"

Translation: "Give me a reason to choose you."

**Response:** *"Honest answer? Most of us are similar on materials and warranty. What's different about us is that we file the claim with you sitting at the table, we attend the adjuster meeting in person, and we don't ask for a dollar until insurance approves the work. So you're never out of pocket beyond your deductible. That's it. No magic."*

Stop selling features. Sell process and skin in the game.

## Part 4 — The Claim Walk-Through Script (Kitchen Table)

After the inspection, you sit down at the kitchen table. This is the conversion moment. The script:

> "Here's how this works. You're going to call [their insurance company]. You'll say two things — you had storm damage and you want to file a claim. Don't say 'my roof' — say 'storm damage.' Adjusters route claims differently based on the wording. We want it routed broadly.

> They'll schedule an adjuster to come out, usually within 5–10 business days. I'll be here when the adjuster comes — that's normal and they expect contractors to be present. It actually helps the adjuster do a more thorough scope.

> If the claim is approved, your insurance will write you a check minus your deductible. We handle everything else — the supplements, the materials, the install. Your only out-of-pocket is the deductible.

> Worst case: they deny it. Costs you nothing, no obligation. Best case: we get you a roof and we'll see you back here in 3–4 weeks for the install.

> Want me to help you make the call right now while we have time? It takes 8 minutes."

The phrase **"Want me to help you make the call right now"** is the close. About 50% of homeowners say yes if the inspection was solid. The other 50% say "let me think about it" — you book a callback for 48 hours.

## Part 5 — The 72-Hour Storm Response Playbook

When hail hits, your entire operation needs to mobilize. Here's the play:

### Hour 0–6 (Intelligence)
- Pull NOAA hail maps the moment the storm clears. Look for stones ≥1.25" — that's the minimum threshold for confident damage.
- Identify the 3–5 hardest-hit zip codes.
- Pull aerial maps. Identify neighborhoods that are roof-rich (single-family detached, older builds, high density).
- Cross-reference with your existing customer database for referrals.

### Hour 6–24 (Marketing)
- Adjust paid campaigns: pause everything outside the storm zip, push budget into the affected zips on Meta + Google.
- Launch a geofenced Facebook ad with a "Free storm damage check" creative — landing page is a calendar-booking form.
- Mail postcards to the top 3 zips (yes, postcards still work for storm — they generate 4–8x ROI in the first 30 days).
- Update your Google Business Profile with a storm response post.

### Hour 24–72 (Boots on the Ground)
- Deploy canvassing crews to the worst-hit neighborhoods first, in pairs.
- Use the 7-Question Storm Knock. Goal: 1 inspection booked per 8 doors knocked.
- Triage immediately. Inspections within 24 hours when possible. Speed wins the claim — adjusters get assigned chronologically.
- Set up a war-room. Owner, sales manager, and one ops person, daily 8am stand-up for the first 2 weeks.

### Day 4–14 (Compound)
- File every claim within the first week. Adjusters get backed up after week 2.
- Send "I was your neighbor" letters to homes that didn't open the door — convert week 2 and 3 leads.
- Get on the adjuster schedule. Be present at every appointment.
- Track everything in CRM: door knocked → inspection booked → inspected → claim filed → adjuster scheduled → claim approved → contract signed → installed.

If you execute the full 72-hour play after a Class 4 hail event in a 50K-population city, you should net **80–150 inspections, 40–80 claims filed, 25–50 signed contracts** in the first 30 days.

## Part 6 — Reps Hiring & Onboarding (Storm-Specific)

Storm sales reps are a different breed than retail reps. Hire for these traits, in order:

1. **Composure under rejection.** Knock 40 doors, get 36 nos. The right rep doesn't take it home.
2. **Curiosity about the homeowner.** Best reps ask 4x more questions than they answer.
3. **Comfort with insurance vocabulary.** ACV, RCV, supplements, depreciation. If they can't learn the words in 2 weeks, they won't make it.
4. **Physical stamina.** It's 8 hours on your feet in 95°F humidity.
5. **Ethics.** Storm sales tempts manipulation. Your rep's behavior on door 200 of the day is what you're betting your reputation on.

**Onboarding the first 30 days:**
- Week 1: shadow Tyler (or your top rep) on every appointment. Watch, don't talk.
- Week 2: do the door knock, but Tyler runs the kitchen table.
- Week 3: do the door knock + inspection, Tyler still runs the close.
- Week 4: full appointment solo, Tyler reviews recording after.

Reps who can't ride this glide path in 30 days don't belong in storm sales. Cut them. Not because they're bad people — because the wrong-fit rep in storm sales burns territory you can't get back.

## Part 7 — KPIs That Actually Matter

If you're tracking more than these 7 numbers, you're tracking noise:

1. **Doors knocked per rep per day.** Floor: 80. Target: 120.
2. **Inspections booked per 100 doors.** Target: 12–15.
3. **Inspections completed per booked.** Target: 80%+ (no-show rate < 20%).
4. **Claims filed per inspection.** Target: 55%+.
5. **Approved claims per filed.** Target: 80%+ (lower means you're filing weak claims; coach to higher selectivity).
6. **Contracts signed per approved claim.** Target: 90%+ (this is mostly about rep follow-through).
7. **Cycle time from door knock to install.** Target: ≤ 45 days.

Review these weekly. The minute one of these breaks, drill in. The other 30 metrics your CRM offers will distract you.

## Part 8 — The Mindset Riff

I'll close this playbook with what I tell every new rep on their first ride-along:

You are not selling roofs. You are giving people information. Some of them have a $25,000 problem they didn't know about. Some of them don't. Your only job at the door is to find out which one they are, and then help them either way.

If you walk away from a door feeling slimy, you did it wrong. If you walk away feeling like an inspector, you did it right — whether or not you got the appointment.

The reps who internalize that close more than the reps who try to "win" the door. Every time.

Welcome to storm sales. Go get to work.
